Hazard Detector

As more people ride scooters around campus, a pothole or curb can appear with little time to react. For IEEE Quarterly Projects in spring 2026, our four-person team built a camera-based prototype that spots hazards ahead and warns riders when those hazards are in their path.

Source on GitHubProject writeup on Devpost

What it does

The system processes video from a camera to find people, vehicles, animals, potholes, rocks, stairs, curbs, and speed bumps. It checks whether each detection is close enough and within the scooter's path before playing an audio alert. A browser dashboard shows the video and detections, and a CSV log records them with timestamps.

The Team

Early on, we split the project into four components: hazard logic, custom object detection, path detection, and alerts. We then brought the parts together into one app.

  • Kenny: Used MobileSAM to identify the road surface so the system can filter out objects beside the rider's path.
  • Toby: Built the logic that decides when a detected object is a threat and integrated the components into the web app.
  • Ashwin: Trained custom models for potholes, rocks, stairs, curbs, and speed bumps using public and collected data.
  • Katherine: Built the audio alerts, a two-second cooldown to limit repeat beeps, and timestamped CSV logging.

What I wrote

I chose to work on path detection. In webapp/engine.py, my MobileSAM code segments the road ahead and returns a polygon around the likely route. The hazard logic uses that polygon to ignore detections off to the side. I also set up the repository and built an early YOLO and OpenCV demo before the team integrated our components.

How it works

  1. YOLOv8n detects people, vehicles, and animals. Five custom models detect potholes, rocks, stairs, curbs, and speed bumps.
  2. My code prompts MobileSAM with three points near the bottom of the frame, where the road is likely to be. It keeps the largest returned mask as a road polygon. Segmentation runs every fifth inference frame, and the polygon is reused between updates.
  3. For each detection, the hazard logic checks its size and position against the road polygon to decide whether to warn the rider. The alert system plays a sound, records the detection, and uses a cooldown to avoid constant beeping.

Challenges and lessons

Road hazard datasets were often small or inconsistently labeled, and training on Apple Silicon was slow. Combining several models also caused misclassifications. The team adjusted confidence thresholds and moved training to Google Colab for GPU access.

Integrating YOLO, OpenCV, and MobileSAM into a single webcam prototype taught us that detection accuracy is only part of the problem: alerts also need to be selective enough for a rider to use.

What's next

The current version is a software prototype. A future version could pair a compact camera with a small computer mounted on the scooter, with more work on speed, mounting, and everyday usability.
