Hazard Detector

A camera on an e-scooter streams video into a computer-vision pipeline that spots potholes, rocks, curbs, stairs, speed bumps, people and vehicles, and beeps only when one is actually in the rider's path. I built the part that finds the path: a MobileSAM model that outlines the road surface ahead.

The alert rule

Three checks before it beeps

A detector that beeps at every car in frame is useless, because most of them are parked or far away. An object only counts as a hazard when it is big enough to be close, sitting on the road ahead, and low in the frame. Drag the box around this frame from our test ride, or resize it from the corner, to see the rule decide.

Object class
Close enoughBox covers more of the frame than the class threshold: 15% for people, 22% for vehicles, 8% for ground hazards.
In the pathThe bottom-centre of the box, where the object meets the ground, lands inside the road outline.
Low in frameThe bottom edge is in the lower 40% of the frame, so the object is near the scooter, not down the road.
No alert
My part · path detection

Finding the road with three seed points

Before path detection, the "in path" check was a fixed strip down the middle of the frame. That breaks on curves and whenever the camera tilts, which happens constantly on a scooter.

Segment Anything models take prompts: give them a point and they return the mask of the object under it. The pixels just in front of the front wheel are almost always road, so I seed MobileSAM with three points along the bottom edge, at 2/5, 1/2 and 3/5 of the width. Of the masks it returns, I keep the largest one and turn it into a polygon. The rest of the app can then test any point against it with cv2.pointPolygonTest.

Segmentation is the slowest model in the pipeline, so it runs on every fifth inference frame and the polygon is reused in between. The road ahead barely changes in a sixth of a second.

webapp/engine.py · _update_path_polygon

points = [[2 * w // 5, h],      # left of the wheel
          [w // 2,     h],      # straight ahead
          [3 * w // 5, h]]      # right of the wheel
results = self._sam.predict(frame, conf=0.25,
                            points=points, verbose=False)
if results[0].masks and results[0].masks.xy:
    self._path_polygon = max(results[0].masks.xy,
                             key=cv2.contourArea)

How it developed

I prototyped on a still image first, then on recorded ride video, then reduced the output to the single polygon the hazard logic needs before merging it into the team repo.
